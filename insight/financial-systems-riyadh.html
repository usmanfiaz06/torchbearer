Insight · White paper

Designing financial systems for agency, resilience and productive participation

Saudi Arabia has put an account in the hands of four in five adults and moved most of its shopping off cash. That race is close to won. Findings from the Torchbearer roundtable in Riyadh on what comes next.

White paper Saudi Arabia 13 pages

Read the white paper (PDF) Download

The Kingdom has built some of the best financial plumbing in the world. What it has not yet built, at the same standard, is the experience of using it.

On 10 October 2026, Torchbearer and Siraaj brought bankers, fintech builders, product leads, an educator and investors into one room in Riyadh to ask a single question: Saudi Arabia has given almost everyone access to finance, so what would it take for that access to give people agency, resilience and a way to build something of their own? This paper sets out what the room said, tested against the best public data.

Five findings

  • Access is no longer the bottleneck. Use is. Accounts and digital payments are near universal. Saving, planning and borrowing well are not.
  • There is a missing rung on the credit ladder. Buying a phone in four instalments takes seconds. Borrowing a small sum of cash in an emergency, at a fair price, is still hard. Family is the first call for 38% of adults; own savings for 22%.
  • Productive credit is the multiplier. Only 9% of adults borrowed to run a business, and SME lending sits at about 11.5% of bank portfolios against a 2030 target of 20%.
  • Rules are being read as ceilings. SAMA issues guidelines. Too often institutions turn them into the slowest possible process and call it compliance.
  • The youngest users are slipping. Account ownership among 15 to 24 year olds fell from 71% to 65% between 2021 and 2024.

Six moves to 2030

The paper closes with six practical recommendations, each workable inside the current regulatory framework: measure use rather than ownership, build a fair emergency credit product, lend against cash flow, govern AI credit models early, test journeys on real people before launch, and start financial inclusion at twelve. A scorecard of ten indicators sets out how to tell whether access is turning into outcomes.
