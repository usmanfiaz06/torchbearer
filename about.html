About

A social enterprise
built around delivery

Torchbearer exists because the gap in climate and social work is rarely intent, and rarely money. It is the capability to get a programme designed properly, delivered in a difficult place, and measured well enough that the result means something afterwards.

Sustainability. Inclusion. Impact. — the three tests every programme has to pass

What we are

Delivery-led,
not advice-led

We are a social enterprise working on climate-linked social problems. That phrase matters to us in a specific way: the problems we take on sit where environmental pressure and social exclusion meet, and they are the problems that ordinary service provision tends to route around.

We advise because we deliver. Our authority on how a programme should be designed comes from having run programmes in places where the assumptions in most designs fall apart — where the supply chain is three days long, where the school has no water, where the household that most needs the intervention is the one least likely to be on any list.

We work as an enterprise rather than a charity because the model has to carry its own weight. Programmes that depend entirely on the next grant cycle are programmes with an expiry date attached, and communities can tell the difference.

How we
hold ourselves

Six operating principles. They are on this page because we would rather be held to them in public than describe them in a meeting.

Measure before you claim

No outcome is reported without a baseline it can be compared against. Where we could not establish one, we say so in the reporting rather than implying a change we did not observe.

Local delivery is not a cost decision

Programmes run through local organisations and locally recruited teams. Work that depends on outside staff stops the day the outside staff leave, and that is a design failure, not a funding one.

Distribution is never the intervention

Handing over a product changes a number on a spreadsheet. Product plus education plus follow-up changes behaviour. We do not run the first and report it as the second.

Dignity is a design constraint

How a programme is delivered decides whether people participate in it a second time. We do not photograph distress, we do not publish images without consent, and we do not ask people to perform need in order to receive help.

Say what did not work

Every programme review we publish carries what underperformed and why. A body of work with no failures in it is a body of work that has not been examined.

Build capability, not dependency

The end state of a good programme is a community and a local partner who can run it without us. We plan for our own exit from the first design document.

Governance

The unglamorous infrastructure that decides whether an organisation can be trusted with a programme, a community, or someone else's money.

Safeguarding

Every programme carries a written safeguarding plan before delivery begins, covering child and vulnerable adult protection, reporting routes, and the conduct standard expected of every person working on our behalf — including partner staff and volunteers. Reports can be made outside the programme's own management line.

Data protection

Participant data is collected on the minimum-necessary principle, stored under access control, and never shared with a funder or partner in identifiable form. Consent is taken in the participant's own language, separately for participation and for any use of imagery, and can be withdrawn at any point.

Procurement and conflicts

Procurement above a defined threshold is competitively tendered and documented. Any interest held by a member of staff or the board in a supplier, partner or beneficiary organisation is declared and recorded, and that person is removed from the relevant decision.

Financial control

Programme funds are tracked against a per-programme budget with segregated approval and payment authority. We publish a statement of how funds were applied for every completed programme, and support any independent audit a partner requires.

Independence of measurement

The people who deliver a programme do not sign off its results. Measurement is designed at the scoping stage, held separately from delivery, and — where a partner requires it or the programme's scale warrants it — verified by a third party with no stake in the outcome.

Legal status

Torchbearer is constituted as a social enterprise. Full registration details, constitutional documents, policy set and insurance position are provided to partners as part of any due diligence process, and are available on request.

People

Programmes are only as good as the people who run them, and the honest test of an organisation is whether it will tell you who those people are.
