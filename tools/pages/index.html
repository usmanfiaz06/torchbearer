Our method

Stage 01Scope

We start by establishing what is actually true. Who is affected, how many, and what has already been tried in the same district by someone else. Most failed programmes fail here — they are designed against an assumption rather than a baseline.

Scope closes with a written problem statement and an explicit list of what we will not be able to measure.

Stage 02Design

Programme architecture is written against named targets — SDG indicators and the national frameworks a partner already reports into — so that results arrive in a format the partner can use without translation.

Every design carries a safeguarding plan, a data-protection position and a stated cost per outcome before a single unit is procured.

Stage 03Deliver

Delivery runs through local organisations and locally recruited teams. This is a deliberate constraint, not a cost measure: programmes that depend on outside staff stop working the moment the outside staff leave.

Distribution alone is never the intervention. Product and education are delivered together, or the outcome does not hold.

Stage 04Measure

Baseline at intake, endline at a fixed interval, on the same instrument, with the same population. Where a control group is feasible, we use one. Where it is not, we say so in the reporting rather than implying a counterfactual we did not test.

Attrition is reported as a number, not absorbed into the result.

Stage 05Verify

The evidence trail is assembled so that a third party can reconstruct the result without speaking to us: raw instruments, method notes, exclusions, and the reasoning behind every adjustment.

This is the stage most implementers skip. It is the one that makes a result usable in a disclosure, an audit or a board paper.

What we are
built to do

Programme design

Problem definition, theory of change, indicator sets and cost-per-outcome modelling, written against the frameworks a partner already reports into.

Field delivery

Execution through local partners in hard-to-reach districts — procurement, logistics, training, community engagement and safeguarding.

Measurement & verification

Baseline and endline instruments, data governance, independent verification, and reporting built for audit and disclosure use.

Advisory & sector capability

Framework design, indicator architecture and delivery-capability building for institutions running their own social and environmental programmes.

Evaluation of existing work

Independent review of programmes already running — what is working, what the data actually supports, and what should stop.
