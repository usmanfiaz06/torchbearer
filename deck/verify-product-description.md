## Full description

### What it is

Torchbearer Verify is a measurement and verification platform for social programmes. It takes a
programme from the design stage, through delivery in the field, to a body of evidence that an
auditor, a regulator or a sceptical board can accept.

It is not a reporting tool that sits at the end of the process. It sits at the beginning, and stays
attached to the programme the whole way through, because that is the only sequence in which a
defensible number can be produced.

### The problem it solves

Social impact has no common unit. Carbon has the GHG Protocol and a tonne means the same thing
everywhere. Social value has no equivalent, so reporting falls back on proxies: people reached,
sessions delivered, training hours, budget disbursed. Every one of those describes activity, and none
of them describes what changed for anybody.

The part that makes this urgent is that it cannot be corrected later. If a programme ran for three
years without recording where its participants started, no analyst can construct that baseline
afterwards. The data does not exist and never will. Measurement has to be designed before delivery,
or the result is not defensible at any price.

Meanwhile the requirement is tightening. Sustainability disclosure obligations are arriving on a
visible trajectory, the social pillar is the one organisations are least able to evidence, and
assurance is moving from voluntary to expected. A growing number of institutions are about to be
asked to prove social outcomes to a standard their current reporting does not meet.

### How it works

**Indicator builder.** A programme is defined against named external targets, SDG indicators and
Vision 2030 or Saudi Green Initiative pillars, rather than against internally invented metrics that
have to be translated later. The system generates the baseline instruments and the endline schedule
before delivery starts, and states in writing which intended outcomes will not be measurable, and
why. That last output is the one clients do not expect and value most.

**Field capture.** Collection that works where the programme actually runs: offline, on low-cost
devices, in the participant's own language, through local delivery partners. Attrition, exclusions
and non-contacts are recorded as they occur, timestamped, rather than reconstructed at the end from
memory. Data governance is built in: minimum necessary collection, access control, and no
identifiable participant data passed to funders or partners.

**Evidence pack.** At programme close the system assembles the raw instruments, the sampling and
method notes, the exclusions, and the reasoning behind every adjustment, into a package a third party
can use to rebuild the result independently. It is formatted for the reporting framework the client
is already subject to, so the output arrives in the language they report in rather than in ours.

### Why it is different

Reporting platforms aggregate whatever data they are given. They cannot repair a missing baseline,
because no software can. Consultancies design measurement frameworks but do not deliver, so their
instruments are written by people who have never had to run one in a village with no reliable power.
Implementing organisations deliver but design measurement after the fact, which is the point at which
it can no longer be fixed. Assurance firms arrive at the end, when the evidence either exists or does
not.

Verify does all three, and it was built outwards from field delivery, which is the only order that
works. You can add measurement discipline to a delivery organisation. You cannot add delivery
credibility to a software company.

The defensible part is not the code. It is that the code encodes a method proven in genuinely
difficult conditions, and that we keep delivering programmes, so the method stays honest.

### Who it is for

**Corporates facing sustainability disclosure** with a social pillar they cannot currently evidence.
They have the mandate, the budget and a named person accountable, and no delivery capability. This is
the beachhead, because the problem has a date attached to it.

**Non-profits under professionalisation pressure**, now required to demonstrate measurable impact
rather than activity.

**Funds and development finance institutions** that need results inside a defined framework rather
than a narrative report.

**Government and public sector entities** building measurement capability they intend to keep
in-house.

### What it is not

- Not an ESG reporting tool. Those aggregate at the end; Verify starts before the programme does.
- Not a survey app. Data collection tools capture responses; they do not design indicators, set
  baselines, or produce anything an auditor can check.
- Not a dashboard. A dashboard makes existing numbers prettier. Verify decides which numbers exist.
- Not an evaluation consultancy in software form. Evaluations arrive once, late, and cost more than
  the measurement budget.

### The commercial shape

Three lines that build on each other. A fixed-scope **design engagement** that produces the
measurement plan and the cost-per-outcome model, deliberately small because it is the document that
makes the platform obviously necessary. A per-programme **licence** for Verify, which is the recurring
line and the reason this is a venture rather than a consultancy. And **delivery and verification**,
either full execution through local partners or independent verification of programmes already
running.

Nobody buys measurement software until they have seen what their current numbers cannot survive. The
sequence matters more than the pricing.

### Current status

The method is finished and published in full: five stages with a standard attached to each, and
twelve analytical papers where every figure is cited to source. It has been run on our own programmes
in Pakistan, where supply chains are long, service coverage is thin, and the households that most need
an intervention are on nobody's list. A measurement approach that survives there transfers downwards
in difficulty rather than upwards.

What does not exist yet is the software. The indicator design, field capture and evidence pack are
currently assembled by hand for each programme, which works and does not scale. Turning that manual
method into a product is the specific piece of work in front of us.

## Phrases that carry the argument

Use these; they are the ones that land.

- "Prove what changed, not what you spent."
- "A number without a method is an opinion."
- "A baseline nobody collected cannot be recreated afterwards."
- "Reporting tools aggregate whatever they are handed. They cannot fix a missing baseline."
- "We are selling the discipline that makes the number exist in the first place."
- "You can add measurement to a delivery organisation. You cannot add delivery credibility to a
  software company."
- "An evidence trail complete enough that a sceptic can disagree with us using our own data."

## Words to avoid

Leverage, empower, holistic, cutting-edge, revolutionise, seamless, end-to-end synergy, game-changing,
robust solution, unlock potential. Every one of them signals that the writer had nothing specific to
say. The product's whole claim is specificity, so the language has to match it.
